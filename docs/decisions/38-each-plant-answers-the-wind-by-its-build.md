# Each plant answers the wind by its build

Round 16 made one system move every plant (decision 36), but the wind still
pushed each fluttering vertex along its own normal and held every level's
bend at one angle however long the piece. A flower's three-sided stem and its
bells opened into broad fins in a gust, and a willow's 3 m strand flapped as
one leaf. Now:

- Every level turns about one axis per joint, so whatever a joint carries
  turns as one: a leaf, a bell or a flower head keeps its shape, and a bough
  curves without creasing. A turn grows smoothly from its joint and eases
  toward a most it never passes.
- Leaves and flowers turn on their stalks; stems and moss, which carry or
  cling, do not flutter, and neither do a fir's stiff needles.
- Past where its turn holds, a hanging piece swings less and trails, so a
  willow's strands sway rather than whip.
- A taller plant's trunk and boughs lean less, because it is thicker.
- The flora contract test holds every plant, great tree and drift of
  wildflowers to its shape in the gustiest world's strongest gust: no
  triangle stretches or shrinks by more than a quarter, or turns over.

In the gustiest world, a great willow's strands now travel about 2.1 m at
up to 1.8 m/s where they travelled 6.5 m at up to 18.6 m/s.

## Why

The reviewer, after round 16: "the wind makes the flowers completely
unintelligible now" and "some of the areas react so insanely to it that it
looks unrealistic. Willows blow in crazy ways." After round 2's board, they
chose to keep how far plants move as the board showed it, and asked for
ordinary wind to be subtler and less regular, which is the wind field's
(the World session's).
