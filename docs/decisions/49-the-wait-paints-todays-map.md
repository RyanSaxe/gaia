# The wait paints today's field map, and shows nothing pending

While a world opens, the waiting sheet paints the field map as it is today:
its paper, torn edge and lantern, with no fringe of wood, title or
turned-down corner. Color is health, in the map's own colors. Each file's
health is painted once it is settled, whenever that is: today most files'
health follows from the code facts and is painted as soon as the land is
divided, and the rest is painted as Jev's answers come in. The sheet never
shows a color it later changes, and it needs no change as Jev's part in
health grows. Each area's name is lettered in the map's survey capitals once
its last answer is in. While the world bakes, the pen draws each building's
and landmark's mark at its laid-out place, one by one. When the bake
returns, the hill shade, water, contours, trails and trees come in, and the
paper folds away into the world.

Nothing else moves. There is no brush, no clear water and no drifting light:
the only motion is real things arriving on the sheet, and a lull is a still
map.

For this, the world service's messages carry every file's patch with the
land and each file's health as it settles (`world.progress` stages `land`
and `health`, a `contract:` change).

## Why

On round 6's board the reviewer chose health bleeding in as answers come,
names as each area settles, and the marks and then the relief during the
bake. The board had cheated: it showed each file's final health when Jev
answered, though most files' health was known from the start and some
answers would change it. In the thread he agreed that each file's health is
painted when it settles, whenever that is. Of the brush, the clear water and
every other sign that work was still pending: "they all look so fake ... I
think nothing is probably right."
