# Nothing reminds you it is an app

Gaia's first value is immersion: a person is in a consistent world, and
nothing on the screen reminds them it is a program. No click rings, no
compass bar, no text floating over the land, no labelled boxes, no hover
highlights, no jargon and no status text. What a person needs to read
comes as the world's own things: signs and fingerposts, slips of paper,
the field map. The same value covers how the world behaves: things obey
gravity, nothing pops in or out as the person walks, and the world never
lags or settles in a way a person could notice. Smoothness on the
computer is the bar; the phone may run choppier, and is not optimized
further.

## Why

The reviewer, in round 14: "I really care about the game feeling like a
consistent world people are in and not reminding them its a game. this is
why I care about gravity. how things look. lack of lag. etc etc." And on
smoothness: "if the world lags or calibrates in a way that is EVER human
perceptive, it's not okay."
