# Grass, ground and water answer vitality

Until now the land itself never declined: trees, buildings, trails and
landmarks fell to ruin on lush green grass, and all of a world's water
shared one vitality. Now:

- The ground takes the vitality of whatever it belongs to, as the trees do:
  a file's patch its file's, an area's own ground (its lots) its area's,
  pooled from every file under it, and the wild past the land always
  thrives. A dead grove in a healthy area is one sick patch in a living
  place, and a ruined area is ruin all through.
- Health eases across every border, over about 7 m between patches and
  8 m at the land's rim, so no edge shows as a line.
- As ground declines, its grass thins, shortens and turns to straw, its
  cover dries and bare earth opens in patches; ground above 0.85 looks as
  it always did.
- Water follows the area it flows through: clear and lively when it thrives,
  a still brown murk when it fails.
- The scattered rocks, bushes and flowers read the same field, so they ease
  across borders with the grass beneath them.
- A change in vitality rewrites a small texture and never rebakes, as
  everywhere else.

## Why

The reviewer approved the rule: "In a failing area the grass thins and
yellows, bare ground shows, and the brook clouds, so ruin reads in the land
itself, not only in trees and buildings; vitality per area instead of one
value for the whole world's water." And in its thread: "the ground takes the
vitality of whatever it belongs to, exactly as the trees do. The patch under
a failing file's grove thins and yellows with its trees, the neighbouring
files' patches stay green, and an area's own ground follows the area."
