# The journal shows every score as a percentage, and leads each page with its best and worst

A page shows its quality and its importance as percentages, every row its
quality, and the caption each concept. Only numbers, never level words.
A file's first page leads with snippets of its best and worst
definitions, and a folder's with its best and worst files, chosen by
quality weighed by importance, so a tiny helper never leads and a file's
heart can be its worst. Each snippet is a definition's first line and one
block, the rest folded. Jev is asked which block is key and which most
needs work, and not which is finest. A long definition opens on what most
needs work when it is below the world's median quality, else on its key
block.

## Why

The reviewer: "I genuinely could not care less about the idea of an
importance word or words about quality lol I only care about the number",
and "Convert numbers to percentages to be clear that it's within that 0-1
range". Ranked by quality alone, the best part was almost always a tiny
helper. Asked for both, Jev named the same block finest and most in need
in 56% of Gaia's definitions, and the reviewer: "yeah you can get rid of
'finest'".
