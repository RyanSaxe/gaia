# One way to touch the world

Touching the world only ever moves the person. A tap or click on the land
walks there; on a thing it walks up to the thing, the view turns gently to
frame it beside its card, and the card opens. A drag looks; keys walk.
Everything that is not movement is paper opened from the corner controls
(the map and the slip), and while that paper is open the world waits: a tap
on it folds the paper and moves no one. A card belongs to its thing and
closes when the person walks away from it. The three ways of knowing where
you are (arrival titles, the field map, markers with a compass) show
together, and the slip no longer chooses between them: it carries Gaia's
mark, how to wander, and the way back to the lab.

## Why

The reviewer, on round 11's immersive world: "This is a bit too much where
UX is bad because clicking things sometimes move you and sometimes do other
things." Clicking beside the open map walked the person instead of folding
it, a tap with the slip open both folded it and walked, and a card stayed
open on a thing left far behind. They chose all three ways of knowing where
you are together.
