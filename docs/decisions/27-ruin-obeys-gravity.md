# Ruin obeys gravity

A ruin may be as broken as it likes, but nothing in it floats. Every piece
of a building or landmark knows what holds it up and goes no later than
it, and a piece that goes collapses toward what holds it, so it is never
left in the air at any vitality, even partway through going. What falls
comes to rest on the ground or on something standing, not mid-air and not
sunk out of sight. It is all in the vitality channels: thresholds ordered
by support and pivots on what holds each piece, so nothing is rebuilt
when vitality changes.

`unsupportedAt` in `@gaia/realize` is the check, and a contract test holds
every landmark and building primitive to it, so a new one cannot float a
piece.

## Why

The reviewer, after round 11: "The only thing is to not defy physics. So
some of your low vitality landmarks and stuff technically have floating
bricks which can't happen."
