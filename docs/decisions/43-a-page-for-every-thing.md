# A page for every thing, said in a schema Jev can change

A thing's page is its own sheet of paper at the lower left. It rises from
the bottom edge when the person stops at a thing and sinks when they walk
away; it no longer grows out of the minimap. Every thing that stands for
code has a page: buildings, landmarks, a file's trees, and the stones,
bushes and flowers that functions, classes, constants and types stand as.
Stopping at a smaller thing raises its page only after a short stop, so
walking through a grove or past flowers raises none.

The face is the sketch, washed in the thing's health, with up to two notes
in the traveller's hand tied by pencil leaders to the parts they explain;
then where it lives, its name, and its vitality as a reading such as 0.47,
with no label. The rest is folded up behind the face: a crease at its foot
shows it, a tap swings it down and another folds it up. A tap on the field
map only sends the person to the place; it never opens a page.

What the page says is one typed value, `PageContent`, made by one function,
`describe`: the notes, each tied to a part of the sketch, and the rest as a
list of entries. This session holds the page to that schema and its limits
(at most two notes, each one line; the rest any length), and is not
opinionated about what goes in it. The Engine and Jev session decides that,
and may change the type.

## Why

On round 4's page board the reviewer chose notes on the sketch, a rest that
unfolds, pages for a file's trees, and a tap on the map that goes there.
Asked whether the vitality number or the word means more, he said the
number felt more meaningful. Of which things get a page: "shouldn't it be
literally anything that is related to an object? I mean the jev session will
make so many more things possibly created." Of the slots for what the page
says: "in this session lets not be opinionated on this other than making
sure what belongs here is clearly a schema that the jev session can change
as we genuinely decide what goes here."
