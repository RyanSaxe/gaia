# Solid things stop you; water never does

Tree trunks, rocks, bushes and a house's walls stop a walk, and it slides
along them. Each is the convex hull of its outline on the ground, taken from
what is drawn: a trunk's bark at its base, a rock's or a bush's reach at the
ground. A stone lower than half a meter is stepped over, and flowers are
walked through. A tap plans its way around anything in between and ends at
the nearest reachable place when nothing reaches the spot.

Water never stops a walk. Deep water is swum, slower than wading, with the
eyes easing down to float just above the surface and back up on the far
bank, and a gentle bob while swimming. A tap across a pond swims all the way
across.

## Why

The reviewer, on walking through rocks and bushes while only the cottage's
walls stopped the walk: "This needs to be fixed. Same with tree trunks I
hope." On deep water stopping a tapped walk: "You need to walk through water
generally ... people can swim so I feel like going all the way is good."
