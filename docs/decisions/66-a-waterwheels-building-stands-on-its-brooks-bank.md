# A waterwheel's building stands on its brook's bank

A building whose feature needs running water, a mill's waterwheel, stands on
the bank of its area's stream. The wheel's wall comes up to the water, 0.2 to
0.3 m from it. The house stays dry, and the rest of its yard keeps the margin
every yard keeps. The wheel turns in a pit in the stream bed, 0.45 m below
the water, with the pad no more than 0.3 m above it so the paddles dip in,
and the stream runs on past the pier. Every other building keeps today's
siting. The feature is still offered wherever the area holds running water,
and every such area now has a river with banks that fit. Nothing overrides
Jev's pick.

## Why

The reviewer asked whether real worlds have bank spots where a waterwheel's
building could stand, suspecting none (thread `ed1ebb8e`). Measured on
develop, every watered area had room, but the site rule kept every house
about 4.4 m from the water, so almost no spot qualified. The reviewer: "you
have my permission then to make the necessary fixes for this to work
cleanly."
