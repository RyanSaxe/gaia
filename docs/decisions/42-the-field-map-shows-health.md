# The field map shows health, letters like a survey and draws each kind

On the field map, color is health and nothing else, so the map is the
window into vitality. Every area starts from one healthy green, and each
file's own vitality spreads over the ground around it, about 24 m and
across area borders, into one gradient over the land: green, then gold,
russet and ash as health falls. A color means the same health in every
world, because it follows each file's own number, not its rank. Trees on
the map take the same colors, and the sheet carries no legend.

The sheet bleeds to a bare torn edge on every side: no wood fringe, no
title, no region names, no directory paths, no scale bar and no turned-down
corner; a tap off the sheet folds it. It keeps each area's name, file names
close in and the file underfoot beside the traveller.

Areas are lettered in upright, widely spaced survey capitals, and small
names in italic. A name another area shares carries just enough of its path
above it to tell it apart, worked out from the folder names alone so it
fits any codebase. Each building and landmark kind has its own ink and
watercolor mark, true to its shape in the world and worn by its health; as
Jev comes to compose buildings, each mark will be drawn from the building's
own blueprint. Contours, borders and rivers are drawn at the view's size, so
every feature looks the same on the whole sheet and close in.

## Why

After round 16 the reviewer found the map "improved, but ... definitely
not good enough": the greenery edges, the fluff text. On the boards he chose
a bare torn edge, kept area names, file names, the legend and the file
underfoot, and then, seeing land colors and health share the paint: "for
now we can just have color only be healthy ... it's important that the map
is the window into vitality", which made the legend unnecessary. Of round
2's health looks he said none was good enough and asked for "actual
vitality numbers ... a true gradient across the entire world"; on round 3
he chose the autumn ramp across the whole world, by each file's own number.
He chose survey capitals, the shortest unique name over a rule that only
renamed "src" ("a hardcoded rule that wont apply to all projects"), and a
drawing for each kind; close in, "the river is different" between zoom
levels, so every line is drawn at the view's size.
