# The wild is inked as an old survey, and the map grows to take you in

Past the land the field sheet's paper stays unwashed, and ink draws the wild
as an old survey: contours and hachures from its heights, its thickets where
they stand, tufts where its golden steppe runs and stipple where it runs to
scrub. Every mark comes from the functions the world grows the wild from, in
`@gaia/terrain`, so the map and the world never disagree. Color stays on code
alone, because on the map color means health. The land's painting ends at the
land's square as it always has, and the corners past its rounded rim stay
painted like the land beside them ([decision 42](42-the-field-map-shows-health.md)).

The minimap is never bare: however far a person walks into the wild, and it
has no end, the minimap shows the wild's ink around them. In the wild it
letters no name. Opened from the wild, the field map's sheet grows to frame
the land and the traveller together, reaching 60 m past them; opened on the
land it is the land's square, as before.

The wild cannot be painted onto one sheet, so it is inked in squares anchored
to the world at a ladder of scales, a few milliseconds at a time in the
page's idle time ahead of where the person is and where the map would open,
and kept. A view lays the squares nearest its own scale, crossfading between
two as the map zooms, so nothing is drawn afresh as the person walks.

## Why

With round 3's feedback the reviewer asked not to forget "how we want to
render 'wilds' on the minimap (and what that means when we open the overall
map)". Round 4's board showed today's minimap gone to bare paper past the
land and today's field map standing the traveller on the sheet's edge however
far out they were, then three looks for the wild (ink survey, a faded wash,
the traveller's pencil) and three sheets for the map opened from the wild. A
faded sage wash would read as land gone to seed on a map whose color means
health, and the pencil would have meant storing every walk. The reviewer
chose the ink survey, the sheet that grows to take the traveller in, and no
name in the wild.
