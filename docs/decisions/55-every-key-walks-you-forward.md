# Every key turns you that way and walks you forward

A person always walks the way they face, and each walking key is a way to
walk, taken from where they face when they press it. W or the up arrow
walks ahead. A or the left arrow turns them to face left and walks them
that way, D or the right arrow to the right, and S or the down arrow turns
them around and walks them back the way they came. Vim's k, h, l and j do
the same. They turn at the pace a tapped walk turns them, and walk only as
fast as they face their way, so a way behind them is walked by turning
round and setting off. A key turns from the way the keys already walk, so
left pressed twice turns them around. Holding keys keeps them walking;
letting go stops them.

## Why

Before, A and D (and the left and right arrows) spun the view in place,
and S only stopped a tapped walk, so nothing walked a person back. The
reviewer: "When I press left I dont want to spin, I want to TURN that
direction and walk forward. Same with back, I should turn around and walk
forward. So I am always walking forward."
