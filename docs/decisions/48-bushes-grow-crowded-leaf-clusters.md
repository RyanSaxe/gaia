# Bushes grow crowded leaf clusters

A bush's leaves grow in crowded clusters, a new leaf cut (`CUT.crowded`):
fourteen rounded leaves overlapping around a short stalk. Short twigs from
the stems carry the clusters at three depths, smaller at the rim and larger
inside, faced out and up, with a ragged outline of uneven clumps, notches
and a few stray twigs. Leaves alone fill a bush; nothing dark stands inside
it. Scrub grows low, wiry, arching stems with tufts of clusters at most of
its tips. A feathery shrub keeps its needle sprays.

A tired bush thins early: its clusters drop evenly from vitality 0.75 to
0.25, the outer ones first, so about three in ten are gone by 0.6 and half
by 0.5.

## Why

At screen density a spray's five small leaves cover about 7% of their card,
so the old bushes read as cages of stems: from 2 m the box mound filled 70%
of its outline and the blueberry 65%. Laying three times as many sprays at
three depths still filled only 84% and 75%. Crowded clusters fill 90% and
88%, at fewer triangles for the box mound and the rhododendron and more for
the blueberry, only for its berries. After round 3's board the reviewer said
the rounded bushes over a dark heart "dont really look very good"; on round
4's board, which drew bushes at screen density, they wrote of the leafy rows:
"LOVE THIS. Great job." On round 5's page they chose that a tired bush thin
faster, so it reads as tired from 5 m, over one that browns and thins only
at its edge.
