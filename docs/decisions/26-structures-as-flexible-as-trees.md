# Buildings and landmarks are as flexible as trees

A building's or a landmark's look comes from a few strong axes that
change its whole form, as a tree's skeleton does, not from trim on one
fixed shape. A building is put together from masses: a single block, an
L, a T, a long range or a cluster, with a porch, a lean-to or a turret
built on, one to three storeys that keep one eave line or step down, and
gabled, hipped or half-hipped roofs (a lean-to's one slope, a turret's
cone). Walls, roofs, openings and dressing compose on whatever the plan
lays out, so Jev can make a hamlet of very different buildings from the
same primitives. Landmarks work the same way: a tower's plan, profile,
galleries and crown; standing stones in a ring, an avenue, a dolmen, a
cairn field or a group of menhirs; a great tree's form and age.

Every axis is a closed field Jev fills. Every form is genuinely
procedural, and its ruin follows from it: each mass rots by the same weak
corner, a lean-to caves in, a turret's cone rots through, a stone leans or
falls whole about its foot and never bends.

## Why

The reviewer: "I agree variety is missing. Not just in terms of the amount
of landmarks on objects but their perturbation. One thing amazing about our
trees is their schema is so flexible that Jev can really make a world so
different with the same components. Our structures, land marks included,
need to be the same." Earlier, on decline: it must look broken down,
overgrown and dead, by genuine procedural generation and clean code.
