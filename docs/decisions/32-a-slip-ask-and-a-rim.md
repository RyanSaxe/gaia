# A slip for where you are, a line to ask, a rim to touch

In the immersive world, an area's name is written on a small slip of warm
paper at the lower left as the person arrives, and fades away; standing
still writes the file underfoot on the same slip. Nothing names a place in
the sky. A thing a person walks up to says one line on a slip at the lower
edge, its name and one word for how it fares, and a tap on the line opens
its page of the traveller's journal, with the details behind "more". A
thing under a resting mouse pointer catches a soft rim of light. These are
the only behaviors: the arrival titles in the sky, the land with no words
on the screen, the field-guide card over the world, the journal page that
opened on its own, and turning to the thing's own sign are gone, and so
are the `?way=`, `?card=` and `?heed=` switches. The field map, the
markers, the signs and the fingerposts stay. The Terrain view keeps its
field-guide card in the panel for debugging.

## Why

On round 14's wayfinding page the reviewer chose "A slip" for where you
are and "Ask" for what a thing tells you, and accepted the recommended rim
of light. Earlier in round 14 the reviewer had asked that nothing on the
screen break the sense of being in a world: no text floating in the air,
no app-like labelled blocks. A slip in the map's paper and ink reads as the
traveller's own note, and asking keeps the details off the screen until a
person wants them. The reviewer found the old card confusing because it was
still the default while the options were tried, so the options that lost
were deleted rather than kept behind switches.
