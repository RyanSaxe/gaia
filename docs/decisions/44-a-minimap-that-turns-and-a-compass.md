# A minimap that turns with you, and a compass that points north

The minimap moves to the lower right and turns with the person, so the way
they face is always up. It is about 156 px square and shows about 130 m
of land, the traveller a little below its middle. Its paint thins out over
6 px into 4 px of bare paper at its torn edge, under a lighter shadow than
before, so it parts from green grass without a drawn frame. After 2.5 s in
a new area it letters the area's name across its land in the field map's
lettering for areas, and in the wild it letters nothing: the old name fades
and no name is written, because the wild stands for no directory. It no
longer writes the file underfoot. A tap on its land unfolds the field map,
which turns to north up as it comes. A tap on its outer edge tucks it down
past the screen's foot, leaving its top 50 px with the name and a band of
turning land, and a tap there brings it back.

The minimap carries no N. The inked rose at the top right that opens the
slip becomes a compass instead: a ring of ink ticks that stays put, its top
tick the way you face, and a needle with a vermilion north that alone turns
to point north, on a damped spring that settles within about a second.

## Why

On round 2 the reviewer liked the name on the land but wanted the edge
designed for ("it's often on grass and both map and grass is green, it
blends in a distracting way") and asked that "a user should totally be able
to minimize this", and that the minimap move to the bottom right so a
thing's page has the lower left. Of the N that slid round the turning
minimap's edge in round 3 he said "it looks off", and asked "maybe there
should just be a compass somewhere?"; told that the rose at the top right
could turn: "Yeah make the top right thing a real compass. Smart", and with
no N on the minimap "the edge can probably become more subtle". On round
4 he chose the feathered edge, the needle in a ring and the tucked fold,
and nothing written in the wild. The name is lettered in the field map's survey
capitals rather than the board's italic, so the minimap and the map speak
in one hand.
