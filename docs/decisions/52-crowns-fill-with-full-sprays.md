# Crowns fill with full sprays, and the trees pass

A crown's sprays are full: each card holds the family's leaves crowded
around a short stalk, so it is nearly all leaf, like a bush's crowded
clusters (decision 48). Three new cuts carry them: `CUT.crowdedPointed`,
sixteen slender pointed leaves fanned in two rings; `CUT.crowdedLobed`, a
broad lobed leaf in the middle and six about it; and `CUT.crowdedUmbels`,
seven umbels of five flowers with a few small fresh leaves. An oval-leaved
crown uses `CUT.crowded`. A crown carries a third as many sprays along
each twig and keeps every twig, so it keeps its reach and shape.

With it, the rest of the trees pass:

- Where boughs leave the top of a trunk or limb, each starts as wide as
  the parent ends and narrows to its own width, so no light band shows at
  the fork.
- An old great tree's buttress roots are thin blades that stand tall at
  the trunk and sweep down to the ground, ending where its round roots
  ended.
- A great willow's leaves keep near their own size, so it hangs more,
  narrower strands, and its curtain reads as strands from 18 m to 60 m.

## Why

A spray's five small leaves cover only about 7% of their card, so a crown
looked full only through many overlapping cards, and each card costs its
whole area to draw (round 6's page 'Great trees, lighter'). Full sprays
roughly halve a crown's leaf triangles, and a tree draws 4% to 32%
quicker, a little thinner from 40 m. On round 7's page 'The trees pass'
the reviewer chose a third as many full sprays, boughs as wide as the
trunk, blades and more, narrower strands, each the recommended option.
