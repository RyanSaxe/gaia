# Walk up to a thing to see what it stands for

Every building and tree has a sign in the world with its name: a signboard
at the end of a building's walk, a small plaque at a tree's foot. They are
always there, readable as a person comes near and lit by the lantern at
night, and they weather with what they name.

Tapping a building, a tree or its sign means "walk to it, then show it":
the person walks to a spot in front of it (the end of a building's walk,
just outside a tree's crown), and on arrival a card opens in the panel on
a desktop or the sheet on a phone. The card says what the thing is, its
name, what it does, where it lives in the code, what it leans on and what
leans on it, and its vitality with the signals behind it. A thing already
close is shown at once; a new tap or a key cancels the walk and no card
opens. The view never turns on its own.

## Why

The reviewer chose "Walk to it, then show it" for tapping a tree while
walking, and asked what "show it" means. On buildings: "something that can
be fun is designing the right way of having a user walk up to something
and see what it represents."
