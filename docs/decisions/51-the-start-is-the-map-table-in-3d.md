# The start is the map table in 3D, each world's card is its saved map, and the reviewer's logo heads it

Gaia opens on the traveller's map table in 3D. A sheet folded in four lies
on an oak table with a quill, an inkwell and a brass lantern, all lit by the
world's own sun, sky, moon and lantern at the hour, with the light of
"Meadow morning", since no world is open yet. The paper has fibres, folds,
curled corners and torn edges that catch the light, and the table beyond
the sheet is out of focus. The address is written in ink on the line from
a text field no one sees; while it is asked after, a darker stroke runs
along the line, and an address that leads nowhere is answered under it in
red-brown ink. The start does not say when a world was last walked.

Each world walked lies on the table as a card, a picture of its own field
map in health's colors, saved with the world when it was last opened: once
the world stands and its map is painted, the page paints the whole map and
the world service keeps it with that recent world. A tap on a card lifts
it, and it rises toward the person into the wait's sheet while the table
fades into the wait's paper.

The reviewer's logo, "gaia" in mossy and cracked stone letters, heads the
start's sheet and the slip, wherever Gaia's mark is shown 48 px tall or
more. Today's flat round mark stays as the window's icon and wherever the
mark is drawn small, where the logo would blur.

For the picture, the world service and page contract changed in a
`contract:` commit: a recent world carries `picture`, a WebP data URL, in
place of the postcard of its areas, and the page sends it in a new message,
`world.picture`.

## Why

On round 6's board the reviewer chose the map table for the start, with
each world as a picture of its own field map in health's colors saved when
it was last opened, and nothing saying when a world was last walked. Round 7
offered the table in 3D, on the meadow, as pictures of the 3D table, and
round 6's flat table; he chose the map table in 3D, and played its
prototype. Of his logo he chose "Where it is large": it tells the same story
as the world, moss on the thriving letters and cracked stone on the
weathered ones, but below about 32 px its cracks and moss turn to speckle.

Round 7's board painted the paper on canvases before the first frame and
drew every frame. The built table makes its paper in the shader, draws a
frame only when something changes, and lets go of its renderer once the
wait takes over, so the start costs the world nothing. The board laid out
no more than two worlds; up to six now lie in rows as large as the room
allows, long names broken onto two lines.
